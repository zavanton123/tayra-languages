# Tayra Languages

**Learn a language by reading what you love.**

Tayra Languages turns any text into a reading lesson. Import a story, an article or a book,
read it page by page, tap the words you do not know, and watch the highlights fade as the
words become yours. It runs on Android, iOS, macOS, Windows, Linux and in the browser, and
everything you learn stays on your own device.

## Reading

- **Every word is a button.** Unknown words are highlighted. Tap one to see its meaning, set
  how well you know it, add a translation or a note, and move on. Select several words to
  save an expression such as *à beira* as one term.
- **Five learning levels plus "well known" and "ignored".** Highlights follow the level, so a
  page shows at a glance what is new, what is being learned and what is already yours.
  Right-click (or long-press) a word to mark it as new, or to mark a learned word as well known.
- **Sentence translations under the text.** Turn on *Show translations* and each sentence
  gets its translation in a lighter line beneath it, or side by side in two columns.
  *One sentence per line* lays the text out for easy comparison.
- **Listen to any sentence.** A play button before each sentence reads it aloud and turns into
  a stop button while it plays. The reader's left pane has the speech engine, the voice for the
  book's language and the speed, so they can be changed without leaving the page.
- **A reader that adapts to you.** Focus mode, adjustable font size, line height and text
  width, several colour themes, keyboard shortcuts on desktop, and bookmarks to pick up
  where you left off.
- **Translations under the mouse.** Resting the pointer on any word, in the text, in a term's
  examples or on the examples screen, shows its translation just above it: your saved meaning
  when there is one, otherwise the offline dictionary or the chosen translation engine.
- **Term details without leaving the page.** The side pane shows the word's dictionary
  entry, its base form, example sentences, and links to online dictionaries you choose per
  language.

## Vocabulary

- **A vocabulary list** with search, status filters and sorting, bulk status changes, and CSV
  and Anki export; on desktop, cards can also go straight into a running Anki with the
  AnkiConnect add-on.
- **Parents and components.** Link an inflected form to its base word; the popup shows the
  base word's meaning and, for expressions, the words inside them.
- **Word frequency.** Up to 10,000 of the most common words of the language, a hundred to a band
  and coloured by your status, with your level marked; hover for a translation, click to save.
  For every language the app teaches that has a corpus to count.
- **Pronunciation** with the device's speech voices, and recorded example sentences.
- **Statistics** of words read and learned over time, per language and per book.
- **A home page** with the words added today, your day streak and a year of study as a
  heatmap, and one tap back into your course, your book or your flashcards.

## News

- **The latest news in your language** from about 235 feeds in 68 languages, checked to work: public
  broadcasters and international services (BBC, DW, RFI, RFE/RL, RFA, Yle, NRK, SVT and others),
  Global Voices' translated feature stories, The Conversation's explanatory articles, and easy-language news where it exists (8 Sidor, Yle
  selkouutiset, News in Levels, RFI's Journal en français facile, Nachrichten in Leichter Sprache...).
- **Wikipedia and other wikis in your language**: the articles read most yesterday, or any you
  search for, read by their introduction or whole; it also covers the languages with no news feed
  (Breton, Latin, Manx, Cornish, Sanskrit...). Beside Wikipedia: Simple English Wikipedia, the
  Klexikon and MiniKlexikon (German for children), Wikivoyage's travel guides and Wikibooks.
- Each article is marked easy, medium or hard by the share of its words you know, and opens in the
  reader as a text of its own, kept with the news rather than among your books.
- Filter by source, difficulty, topic, date, easy language, and articles not read yet, or search.

## Translation, online or offline

Sentence translations and suggested meanings come from the engine you pick on the
Translation screen:

| Engine | Needs |
| --- | --- |
| MyMemory | nothing; free, with a daily quota |
| Google Translate | a Google Cloud API key |
| On this device | nothing online after the first download |

Keys are kept in the platform's secure storage: the Keychain on macOS and iOS, the Keystore
on Android, the Data Protection API on Windows. Google Translate has a *Check key* button.

**On this device** works with no network at all. On Android and iOS it uses Google ML Kit,
with one small model per language that you download once. On desktop it uses Argos
Translate, which the app installs into its own folder on first use, models included. When a
model is missing, the reader says which one and offers to install it right there.

## Speech

The play buttons and the speaker icons on terms use the engine picked on the Speech screen:

| Engine | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| System voices | the voices of the operating system; nothing to download here |
| Piper | neural voices that run offline, with downloads for most of the languages the app teaches |
| Kokoro | a high-quality offline voice for English, Spanish, French, Italian and Portuguese |

Piper and Kokoro run on desktop, Android and iOS. Voices are downloaded from the Speech screen,
one per language for Piper and a single model for Kokoro, and the screen lets you pick a voice
per language, set the speed and try a sentence. A language without a downloaded voice falls
back to the system voice. The web version uses the system voices.

## Offline dictionaries

Download a dictionary pack for a language and its meanings appear in the term pane with no
network: definitions, every sense with a one-tap add, and the link from an inflected form to
its base word. Packs exist for 69 languages, with meanings in English, Chinese, French,
German, Greek, Polish, Portuguese, Russian or Spanish where Wiktionary covers the pair, and are
managed on the Dictionaries screen. When a pack lacks a word, the language's rules, ported from
Yomitan, find what it is a spelling or an inflection of ("walked" to "walk", "еще" to "ещё",
"食べました" to "食べる"), so the meaning and the base word still show. FreeDict's bilingual
dictionaries are offered there as well, beside the Wiktionary packs and for pairs Wiktionary does
not cover. Dictionaries of your own, made for Yomitan (.zip), MDict (.mdx) or StarDict (its .ifo,
.idx and .dict files, or an archive of them), can be imported there too; their entries show beside
the pack of their languages.

## Languages

Read in Albanian, Ancient Greek, Arabic, Armenian, Assyrian, Basque, Belarusian, Bosnian,
Breton, Bulgarian, Cantonese, Catalan, Cornish, Croatian, Czech, Danish, Dutch, Egyptian Arabic,
English, Esperanto, Estonian, Farsi, Finnish, French, Galician, Georgian, German, Greek,
Hawaiian, Hebrew, Hindi, Hungarian, Icelandic, Indonesian, Irish, Italian, Japanese, Kannada,
Khmer, Korean, Lao, Latin, Latvian, Lithuanian, Macedonian, Maltese, Mandarin Chinese, Manx,
Mongolian, Norwegian, Old Irish, Polish, Portuguese, Romanian, Russian, Scottish Gaelic,
Serbian, Slovak, Slovene, Spanish, Swedish, Tagalog, Thai, Toki Pona, Turkish, Ukrainian,
Vietnamese, Welsh or Yiddish, with meanings shown in English, Chinese, French, German, Greek,
Polish, Portuguese, Russian or Spanish. Each language comes with a tutorial book on learning it
with the app, written in that language. The Languages page sets the language being learned, the
native language meanings are shown in, and the language of the interface: English, Chinese, French,
German, Greek, Polish, Portuguese (Brazilian), Russian or Spanish.

Right-to-left scripts (Arabic, Hebrew, Farsi, Yiddish, Assyrian) read right to left. Chinese,
Cantonese, Japanese, Thai, Lao and Khmer, written without spaces, are cut into words with the
language's dictionary and word list, as Yomitan does; Korean words are looked up without their
particles and endings.

## Books

- Import e-books (EPUB, FB2, MOBI, AZW3), documents (DOCX, ODT, RTF, PDF), web pages (HTML,
  MHTML), Markdown, plain text and subtitle files (SRT, VTT), or paste a text: each becomes plain
  text, with the book's own title. A zipped book (`.fb2.zip`, `.fbz`) is read through; PDF is not
  read in the browser. On desktop, Android and iOS, a web page's article can be imported by its
  address, without the menus, ads and comments around it, as Firefox's Reader View reads it.
- Find free books without leaving the app: classics from Project Gutenberg, the transcriptions of
  Wikisource in dozens of languages, graded children's readers from the Global Digital Library,
  Global Storybooks' short picture-book stories in about 150 languages, StoryWeaver's graded
  picture books (desktop and Android, as they come as PDFs), and for some languages a
  library of their own: Wolne Lektury (Polish), Aozora Bunko (Japanese), Litteraturbanken (Swedish),
  Project Ben-Yehuda (Hebrew), Arkiv for Dansk Litteratur (Danish), Ganjoor (Persian), Projecto
  Adamastor (Portuguese), Ebooks libres et gratuits (French), TextGrid (German), Liber Liber
  (Italian) and Elejandría (Spanish); each book marked easy, medium or hard by the share of its words you already know, and added to your
  library in one tap with its source on the last line.
- Photos of pages become text too: pick them, or take one with the phone's camera. iOS reads them
  with Vision; Android with ML Kit for Latin, Chinese, Japanese, Korean and Devanagari text and
  Tesseract for other scripts; the desktop with Tesseract. A Tesseract model (1–5 MB) is downloaded
  for a language the first time a photo in it is read, and can be removed under Dictionaries.
- Long texts are split into pages by paragraph or by word count; pages can be edited, added
  or removed later.
- The library shows every book with its language, progress and how much of its vocabulary
  is already known, with search, sorting, filters and an archive for finished books.

## Command line

The desktop app comes with `tayra`, a command-line tool for the same library: add books and
courses, read a page's words with their statuses, change statuses, export to Anki, make and
restore backups. Every command can answer in JSON, so AI agents can use it too. Settings >
Command-line tool puts it on the PATH; [docs/CLI.md](docs/CLI.md) describes it.

## Get it

Releases on GitHub include an Android APK, a macOS disk image, a Windows installer, and
Linux packages (DEB, RPM and a tarball). The desktop app tells you when a new version is out
and installs it in place when you ask (Settings > App updates). The web version runs in a
browser without installing anything, though its library lasts only for the session.

Build it yourself with a recent JDK:

```
./gradlew :desktopApp:run                      # desktop
./gradlew :androidApp:installDebug             # android
./gradlew :webApp:wasmJsBrowserDevelopmentRun  # web
```

For iOS, run `pod install` in `iosApp` once, then open `iosApp/iosApp.xcworkspace` in Xcode.

Details for contributors, from the module layout to how dictionary packs are built, are in
[docs/DEVELOPMENT.md](docs/DEVELOPMENT.md).
